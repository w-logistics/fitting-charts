Free hydraulic fitting size charts

FittingCharts is a shop reference for identifying hydraulic fittings. Each chart is its own page, with the size, the thread, and a short way to tell that fitting from the ones it gets mixed up with. The inch charts use a dash size. The DIN chart uses the tube outside diameter in millimeters and a series letter. If you are holding a fitting and you are not sure which chart to open, start with the identification guide.

The pages are written for mechanics and shop techs who need a number in the bay, on a phone or a tablet. They are reference aids. They are not the SAE or ISO standard, and they are not a pressure rating for a particular brand or hose.

How to use a dash size

Dash number: on the JIC, ORFS, and ORB charts, the dash is the nominal tube outside diameter in sixteenths of an inch. Dash -8 is 8/16 in, which is 1/2 in tube. On the NPT chart, the dash is the nominal pipe size in sixteenths, and that name is not the thread diameter. On the BSP chart, Eaton's dash is the nominal pipe size in sixteenths as well. The thread that goes with a dash is not the same on every fitting family. A -8 JIC is 3/4-16. A -8 ORB is the same 3/4-16 thread, with an O-ring at the shoulder instead of a 37° flare. A -8 ORFS is 13/16-16. A -8 NPT is 1/2-14. A -8 BSP thread is 1/2-14, and its basic major diameter is 0.825 in, not 1/2 in. The DIN 2353 chart is not a dash chart. 12L is light series for 12 mm tube, thread M18 x 1.5. 12S is heavy series for the same tube, thread M20 x 1.5. Match the chart to the fitting in your hand. The identification guide puts those threads side by side.
