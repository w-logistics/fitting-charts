Free hydraulic fitting size charts

FittingCharts is a shop reference for identifying hydraulic fittings. Each chart is its own page, with the size, the thread, and a short way to tell that fitting from the ones it gets mixed up with. The inch charts use a dash size. The DIN chart uses the tube outside diameter in millimeters and a series letter. The tube chart is the steel tube itself: outside diameter, wall, and working pressure. The flange chart is the four-bolt pad, Code 61 and Code 62, which has no pipe thread. The coupler chart is the quick-disconnect end: ISO 7241 Series A and Series B, ISO 16028 flat face, and the ISO 5675 agricultural tip. Assembly torque for JIC, ORFS, and O-ring boss is on the torque chart. If you are holding a fitting and you are not sure which chart to open, start with the identification guide.

The pages are written for mechanics and shop techs who need a number in the bay, on a phone or a tablet. They are reference aids. They are not the SAE or ISO standard, and they are not a pressure rating for a particular brand of fitting or hose. The tube chart prints the design pressure from the Parker and Air-Way tables it names, for the steel tube those tables specify.

How to use a dash size

Dash number: on the JIC, ORFS, and ORB charts, the dash is the nominal tube outside diameter in sixteenths of an inch. Dash -8 is 8/16 in, which is 1/2 in tube. On the NPT chart, the dash is the nominal pipe size in sixteenths, and that name is not the thread diameter. On the BSP chart, Eaton's dash is the nominal pipe size in sixteenths as well. The thread that goes with a dash is not the same on every fitting family. A -8 JIC is 3/4-16. A -8 ORB is the same 3/4-16 thread, with an O-ring at the shoulder instead of a 37° flare. A -8 ORFS is 13/16-16. A -8 NPT is 1/2-14. A -8 BSP thread is 1/2-14, and its basic major diameter is 0.825 in, not 1/2 in. The DIN 2353 chart is not a dash chart. 12L is light series for 12 mm tube, thread M18 x 1.5. 12S is heavy series for the same tube, thread M20 x 1.5. Match the chart to the fitting in your hand. The identification guide puts those threads side by side. The flange chart uses the dash for the nominal flange size. Dash -8 is a 1/2 in four-bolt pad. Code 61 and Code 62 share that dash and do not share the bolt pattern. The hose chart uses the dash for the inside of the hose. On SAE 100R1, 100R2, and 100R12 hose, dash -8 is a 1/2 in bore. That matches the inch of -8 tube, and it is not the outside of the hose. SAE 100R5 is the exception on that page. Wall thickness and working pressure for the steel tube itself are on the tube chart.
