Hydraulic Steel Tube Size and Pressure Chart (SAE J524 / J525)

Inch hydraulic steel tube from 1/8 in through 2 in: the dash size, the outside diameter, common wall thicknesses, and the design pressure Parker publishes for annealed C-1010 tube. The same dash on a JIC fitting or an ORFS fitting is this tube OD. It is not the thread, and it is not the bore of a hose.

Quick rule: The dash is the tube outside diameter in sixteenths of an inch. Dash -8 is 1/2 in OD. The pressure in the tables is for fully annealed C-1010 steel at a design factor of 4, for normal service. A different material, a higher temperature, severe shock, or a weld seam that the spec does not already account for means you derate, or you use another chart.

What the inch pressures assume

Parker Catalog 4300 Table U8 sets the allowable design stress for a design factor of 4, at 72°F, for fully annealed tube. Carbon steel C-1010 is 12,500 psi. The tube specifications on that row are SAE J356, SAE J524, and SAE J525. Table U15 turns that stress into a design pressure with Lamé's formula. Parker says those pressures use severity of service A (design factor 4) and a temperature derating factor of 1.

Industrial Tube and Steel describes SAE J524 as cold drawn and annealed seamless low-carbon steel pressure tubing, and SAE J525 as cold worked and annealed electric-resistance-welded tubing. Both descriptions say the tube is for hydraulic lines and for flaring and bending. Plymouth, which makes SAE J525, describes that tube as formed, welded, and cold drawn, then annealed, in grade C-1010. Parker prints one stress for J524 and J525. There is no second column that lowers J525 because it started as a welded tube. SAE J356 is on the same Parker row. Air-Way's steel chart uses the same 12,500 psi and about the same 4:1 factor, and it names SAE J524, SAE J525, SAE J526, and SAE J356 together. Parker Table U8 does not list J526.

The number in the table is a design pressure, not a burst pressure. The design factor of 4 is already applied. Do not multiply the chart by 4 and then run the tube at that pressure. Parker says the figures are for reference only and are subject to change. Air-Way says the same, and says the values are not guaranteed minimums.

Dash -18 (1-1/8 in) and dash -28 (1-3/4 in) are not rows in Parker Table U15. Those two rows below are the Air-Way steel chart, which uses the same stress and the same 4:1 factor. Every other pressure is the Parker Table U15 figure for Steel 1010. A cell that says "not listed" is a wall that source does not print for that OD. It is not a zero, and it is not permission to calculate your own.

Millimeters next to the OD and the wall are the figures Air-Way prints beside those inch sizes. Parker Table U15 is in inches. For 5/16 in, Air-Way prints 0.312 in and 7.92 mm. Parker calculates that dash as 0.313 in OD. Both are the nominal 5/16 in tube. For 3/16 in, both use 0.188 in, and Air-Way prints 4.77 mm.

Derating for service, temperature, and other materials

Parker says to multiply Table U15 by the factors in Table U10 and Table U11 when the job is more severe than normal service or when the temperature changes. The chart above already uses service factor 1.00 and temperature factor 1.

Parker Table U10 severity of service. Multiply the chart pressure by the derating factor.
Service What Parker calls it Design factor Derating factor
ANormal. Moderate mechanical and hydraulic shocks.41.00
BSevere hydraulic shocks and mechanical strain.60.67
CHazardous application with severe service conditions.80.50

Table U11 is the temperature factor. Parker bases it on allowable design stress from ASME B31.1 (1986). For C-1010 and C-4130 the factor is 1.00 from 100°F through 300°F, 0.99 at 350°F, 0.98 at 400°F, and 0.96 at 500°F. Parker does not print a C-1010 factor above 500°F. Stainless steel, copper, aluminum, and Monel have their own columns. Parker's worked example for 316 stainless at severe service and 500°F is 0.67 times 0.90, which is 0.603. Apply both factors: chart pressure, times the service factor, times the temperature factor.

Air-Way's steel page prints a C-1010 temperature factor of 1.00 from 100°F through 500°F, and it says carbon steel tubing in those ranges does not require derating. The two catalogs do not match at 350°F and above. If you are using the Parker pressures in the tables on this page, use Parker's factors. Stainless steel is not a small step away from these psi numbers. Parker Table U8 puts 304 and 316 at 18,800 psi allowable stress, not 12,500 psi, and the temperature column is different.

A weld is a separate question from the temperature factor. Parker's metric tube bulletin says welded and cold-redrawn tube is usually suitable, and that pressure capability might be reduced because of the weld seam. The inch chart does not already subtract a weld penalty for J525. Tube that is only welded, and is not the annealed spec in Table U8, needs the maker's own rating.

Match the tube OD to the fitting dash

On the JIC chart and the ORFS chart, the dash is the tube outside diameter in sixteenths of an inch. Dash -8 is 8/16 in, which is 1/2 in tube, on both charts. The threads are not the same. JIC -8 is 3/4-16. ORFS -8 is 13/16-16. Read the thread off the fitting chart. This page only names the tube.

The JIC chart on this site lists -2 through -32 and does not list -18 or -28. The ORFS chart starts at -4 and lists -14 and -32 as Parker sizes that SAE J1453 does not include. It does not list -2, -3, -5, -18, or -28. A 1-1/8 in or 1-3/4 in tube still has a dash, 18 and 28. It is not a row on those two fitting charts.

The DIN 24° chart is not a dash chart. The size is the tube OD in millimeters plus the series letter. 12L and 12S are both 12 mm tube, and they do not share a thread. Nothing on that chart is an inch dash.

A hose dash on SAE 100R1, 100R2, and 100R12 hose is the hose inside diameter in sixteenths of an inch. Dash -8 hose is a 1/2 in bore. Dash -8 tube is a 1/2 in outside diameter. The inches match on purpose, and the part you measure does not. SAE 100R5 is the exception on the hose page.

The fitting has its own working pressure. Use the lower of the tube pressure and the fitting maker's rating. Air-Way rates a -8 (1/2 in) 37° flared tube end at 4500 psi, on a 4:1 design factor. Parker rates 1/2 in x 0.049 in C-1010 tube at 2700 psi, so the tube is the lower number on that wall. On a 2 in line the fitting can be the lower number. Air-Way rates the -32 37° flared tube end at 1500 psi. Parker rates 2 in x 0.120 in tube at 1600 psi. Those Air-Way figures are the flared tube end, not the female swivel, which Air-Way rates lower. An ORFS end has its own rating. This page does not copy it.

Wall thickness the fitting will take

Parker Table U12 is the recommended minimum and maximum wall for common fittings. Triple-Lok is the 37° flare, the fitting on the JIC chart. Seal-Lok is ORFS. Ferulok is the flareless bite fitting. A wall outside the range can be the wrong wall for that fitting even when the OD is right. Parker says steel Ferulok is used with seamless or welded and drawn fully annealed tube to SAE J356, SAE J524, or SAE J525, and it names a maximum hardness of RB72 for that tube.

Metric tube, EN 10305-4

These are common OD and wall sizes used with the fittings on the DIN 24° chart. The pressures are not the inch table with the units changed, and they are not the ISO 8434-1 fitting pressures on that chart.

Parker recommends cold-drawn seamless tube, regular annealed (+N), to DIN EN 10305 (Parker still calls this the old DIN 2391) and ISO 3304. The material in this table is E235+N, which Parker also writes as St 37.4 (1.0308). The column is the DIN 2413 III dynamic working pressure, in bar, for dynamic stress up to 120°C. Parker uses a safety factor of 1.5 in that calculation. That is not a 4:1 design factor. The catalog also prints a higher static pressure for loads that do not cycle. This table is the dynamic column only. Corrosion allowance is not included.

The table is seamless tube. Parker says welded and cold-redrawn tube to the same outside dimensions is usually suitable, and that the weld seam might reduce the pressure. E355+N (St 52.4) is a higher-strength Parker table and is not shown here.

FAQ

What do the working pressures in the inch tables assume?

The inch tables are design pressure for fully annealed C-1010 carbon steel tube, with a design factor of 4. Parker Table U8 assigns 12,500 psi allowable design stress, at 72 degrees F, to SAE J356, SAE J524, and SAE J525. Table U15 prints that pressure for severity of service A, which is normal service, and for a temperature derating factor of 1. Air-Way uses the same 12,500 psi and the same 4:1 factor for SAE J524, SAE J525, SAE J526, and SAE J356. The 4 is already in the number. Do not multiply the chart by 4 and call the result a working pressure. The numbers are not a rating for stainless steel, copper, or aluminum, and Parker says they are for reference only, not a guaranteed minimum.

Is welded tube rated lower than seamless tube on this chart?

No separate welded column is printed. Parker gives SAE J524 and SAE J525 the same allowable stress, and Air-Way puts both specs on one chart. J524 is the seamless cold-drawn annealed tube. J525 is welded and then cold-drawn, and it is also annealed. Parker metric tube literature says a weld seam can still reduce the pressure a welded tube will hold. Do not use this chart as a weld derating. If the tube is not the annealed C-1010 spec named above, use the tube maker's rating.

How do I match a tube OD to a fitting dash size?

On JIC and ORFS, the dash is the tube outside diameter in sixteenths of an inch. Dash -8 is 1/2 in tube. The JIC thread for that dash is 3/4-16. The ORFS thread for that dash is 13/16-16. The tube is the same and the thread is not. DIN 2353 uses the tube OD in millimeters and a series letter, not this dash. A hose dash is the hose bore on SAE 100R1, 100R2, and 100R12 hose, which is a different measurement.

When do I derate the chart pressure?

Multiply the chart pressure by Parker's service factor and by Parker's temperature factor. Normal service is 1.00. Severe hydraulic shock is 0.67. Hazardous service is 0.50. For C-1010, the temperature factor is 1.00 from 100 degrees F through 300 degrees F, 0.99 at 350 degrees F, 0.98 at 400 degrees F, and 0.96 at 500 degrees F. Other materials use other factors in the same table. Air-Way's steel chart prints a C-1010 factor of 1.00 from 100 degrees F through 500 degrees F and says carbon steel does not require derating in those ranges. Use the factor from the source whose pressure you are using.

Can I use a heavier wall than the fitting table allows?

The fitting has a minimum and a maximum wall. Parker Table U12 is that range for Triple-Lok 37 degree flare, Ferulok flareless, and Seal-Lok ORFS. A wall thicker than the maximum may not flare or bite correctly. A wall thinner than the minimum is outside the range Parker recommends for that fitting. The pressure the joint can hold is the lower of the tube pressure and the fitting maker's rating.

Are the metric bar numbers a 4:1 rating?

No. Those bars are Parker's DIN 2413 III dynamic working pressure for seamless E235+N tube to DIN EN 10305-4, calculated with a safety factor of 1.5, for temperatures up to 120 degrees C. They are not the 4:1 inch-tube pressures, and they are not the ISO 8434-1 fitting pressures on the DIN chart. E355+N tube is a different Parker table and is not shown here.

Safety note: Tube OD, wall, material, and the fitting all set the pressure a joint can hold. A wrong wall or the wrong fitting is a common cause of hydraulic leaks and failures. Match the tube spec in your hand, then confirm the design pressure with the tube maker and the fitting maker. This page is a reference aid, not a pressure rating for a mixed assembly.
