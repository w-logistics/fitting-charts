ORFS Fitting Size Chart (SAE J1453 / O-Ring Face Seal)

O-ring face seal fittings: dash size, nominal tube OD, straight thread, and the face-seal O-ring Parker lists for that dash. For a phone or tablet in the shop, next to the JIC 37° chart.

Quick rule: The dash size is still the tube OD in sixteenths of an inch, but the thread is not the JIC thread. A -8 ORFS is for 1/2″ tube and uses a 13/16-16 thread. A -8 JIC is 3/4-16.

Thread callouts are the “T UN/UNF” column of the Parker Seal-Lok table on Catalog 4300 page A7. Parker labels that column UN/UNF for every size and does not print a series on each row. 9/16-18 is UNF. 1-14 is a special pitch (UNS), because 1″ UNF is 12 threads per inch, not 14. Sizes -12 and larger on this chart are 12-pitch UN. Dash -6 and dash -8 are listed as 11/16-16 and 13/16-16, with no series suffix. ASME B1.1 has no UNF pitch for 11/16 or 13/16. Sixteen threads per inch on those diameters is the constant-pitch 16-UN series. Nominal major diameter is the basic diameter of the thread callout (11/16 in is 0.6875 in). Parker rounds the same diameters to three places (0.563, 0.688, 0.813, 1.000, 1.188, 1.313, 1.438, 1.688, 2.000, 2.500). A male thread measures slightly under the nominal major diameter. Confirm with a thread gauge.

Parker also lists metric tube ODs that can use the same fitting body (for example 6 mm with -4, and 8 mm or 10 mm with -6). The dash number on this chart is the inch tube size. Use the sleeve or flange the manufacturer specifies for metric tube.

How to Identify an Unknown ORFS Fitting

  1. Look at the male nose. ORFS is a flat face with an O-ring groove in that face. There is no 37° cone.
  2. Look at the female side. The mating piece is a flat face held by a swivel nut. It is not a 37° flare seat and it is not a tapered pipe thread.
  3. Read the dash from the tube OD. Nominal tube OD in sixteenths of an inch is the dash size. Half-inch tube is -8.
  4. Measure the thread. Caliper the male thread OD and count threads per inch, then use the chart on this page. Do not stop at the JIC chart. Several ORFS threads are one step larger than JIC at the same dash.
  5. Rule out O-ring boss. A male ORB fitting carries the O-ring at the shoulder, at the base of the threads, and it screws into a port. The port thread for a given dash follows the JIC series. A -6 ORB port is 9/16-18; a -6 ORFS thread is 11/16-16.

How ORFS differs from JIC and ORB

  • JIC (SAE J514) seals metal to metal on a 37° flare. The threads only hold the joint. ORFS seals with an O-ring compressed between two flat faces. The threads do not seal.
  • Same dash, different thread. -8 JIC is 3/4-16 UNF. -8 ORFS is 13/16-16. See the comparison table below.
  • Same thread, different dash. 9/16-18 is -6 JIC and -4 ORFS. 1-3/16-12 is -14 JIC and -12 ORFS. Matching only the thread can pick the wrong family.
  • ORB (SAE straight-thread port) is a port connection. The O-ring is at the shoulder and seals on a chamfer in the boss. It is not a face-seal tube union. A -12 ORB port is 1-1/16-12, the JIC -12 thread. A -12 ORFS thread is 1-3/16-12.
  • Parker describes Seal-Lok as a flat-face joint for applications where pressure, vibration, and impulse show up, and says the O-ring should be inspected at each disassembly. Working pressure still depends on size, material, and the fitting series. Use the manufacturer’s rating for the part you have.

FAQ

Are ORFS and JIC fittings interchangeable?

No. They seal differently, and the thread for a given dash size is not the same. A -8 JIC fitting is 3/4-16. A -8 ORFS fitting is 13/16-16. Some thread callouts do appear in both families at different dash sizes: 9/16-18 is -6 JIC and -4 ORFS. The sealing surface is still different. Do not assemble a JIC cone to an ORFS flat face.

How do I tell ORFS from an O-ring boss (ORB) fitting?

On a male ORFS fitting the O-ring sits in a groove on the flat nose, and the nut draws that face against a flat-face swivel. On a male ORB fitting the O-ring sits at the shoulder, at the base of the threads, and it seals against a chamfer in a port. ORB port threads follow the JIC dash series, not the ORFS series. A -6 ORB port is 9/16-18. A -6 ORFS thread is 11/16-16.

What O-ring does an ORFS fitting use?

Use the face-seal size for that dash, not an ORB boss ring. Parker Catalog 4300 lists 2-011 for -4, 2-012 for -6, 2-014 for -8, 2-016 for -10, 2-018 for -12, 2-021 for -16, 2-025 for -20, and 2-029 for -24. The standard compound in that table is 90-durometer nitrile (NBR). Inspect the O-ring when the joint is opened and replace it if it is cut, flattened, or the wrong compound for the fluid.

Which ORFS dash sizes are in SAE J1453?

Parker Catalog 4300 states that Seal-Lok meets SAE J1453 and ISO 8434-3, and it lists -4, -6, -8, -10, -12, -16, -20, and -24 in that series. The same catalog also lists -14 (7/8 in tube, 1-5/16-12) and -32 (2 in tube, 2-1/2-12) and notes that sizes 14 and 32 are not included in SAE J1453.

Does the ORFS thread seal the joint?

No. ORFS uses a straight thread to draw two flat faces together. The seal is the O-ring trapped between those faces. A missing or pinched O-ring will leak even when the threads are tight.
