Hydraulic Hose Dash Size Chart (SAE J517)

Dash size for common hydraulic hose: the nominal inside diameter in sixteenths of an inch, from -3 through -32. The same dash number on a JIC fitting is the tube outside diameter, not the outside of the hose.

Quick rule: Dash -8 hose is 8/16 in inside, which is 1/2 in. Parker Catalog 4400 writes the rule as 4/16 in = 1/4 in = -4. The outside diameter is not the dash. It changes with the braid or spiral, and with the brand.

How to measure a hose

  1. Read the layline. Gates says the replacement size is the size printed on the hose. The layline usually has the dash, the inside diameter, and the SAE type, such as 100R2AT.
  2. If the layline is gone, cut the hose and measure the inside. Gates says to measure the inside diameter. On an assembly, measure the overall length and the fitting orientation before you cut. A caliper across the cut bore is the measurement. Compare it with the nominal ID in the chart above.
  3. Do not use the outside diameter as the dash. Parker says the outside diameter matters for clamps and for a tight envelope, and that you take it from the spec for that hose. Two -8 hoses can differ by more than a tenth of an inch outside. See the -8 comparison below.
  4. Check the fitting separately. The thread on the hose end is a fitting dash, not a hose OD. Use the JIC chart, the ORFS chart, or the identification guide.

Hose dash and fitting dash

On JIC, ORFS, and ORB, the dash is the tube outside diameter in sixteenths of an inch. On true-bore hose, the dash is the hose inside diameter in those same sixteenths. The numbers line up on purpose. A -8 hose has a 1/2 in bore, and a -8 JIC hose end is the fitting made for that hose. The JIC thread is still the thread for 1/2 in tube, which is 3/4-16, not a 1/2 in thread.

The nominal major diameter of a -8 JIC thread is 0.750 in. That is the male thread, not the hose. Parker 422-8 lists a hose OD of 0.81 in. Parker 302-8 lists 0.88 in. None of those is 1/2 in, and none of them is the thread.

An ORFS hose end with the same dash uses the ORFS thread, which is larger than JIC at that dash. Dash -8 ORFS is 13/16-16. See the ORFS chart. An O-ring boss port uses the JIC thread at the same dash, with the O-ring at the shoulder. See the ORB chart.

A pipe-thread hose end uses the pipe-size dash. Gates explains its MegaCrimp part G25100-0808 as a male pipe coupling with a -8 (1/2 in) stem and a -8 (1/2 in) thread. That thread is 1/2 NPT, which is 1/2-14. The thread diameter is not 1/2 in. Use the NPT chart for the thread, and this page for the hose bore.

SAE 100R5 is the exception

Gates says the dash on SAE 100R5 hose (C5C, C5CXH, C5D, C5E, and C5M), and on C14 and PolarSeal, is based on the tubing outside diameter that has the same bore as the hose. The bore is one step smaller than the true-bore dash. Parker 201 is marked SAE J517 100R5. Parker says SAE spec certification for 201 hose is for -4 through -12 only, so the table stops there. The inch sizes match the Gates C5 column.

FAQ

What does a hose dash size mean?

On SAE 100R1, 100R2, and 100R12 hose, the dash is the nominal inside diameter in sixteenths of an inch. Parker Catalog 4400 states the rule as 4/16 in = 1/4 in = -4. Dash -8 is 8/16 in, which is 1/2 in inside. Gates prints 12.7 mm for that size. The outside diameter is a different number and depends on the hose construction.

Is a -8 hose the same as a -8 JIC fitting?

The inch matches, and the part you measure does not. Dash -8 hose has a 1/2 in bore. A -8 JIC fitting is also for 1/2 in tube, and its thread is 3/4-16, with a nominal major diameter of 0.750 in. The hose outside diameter is neither of those. Parker 422-8 lists an OD of 0.81 in. Parker 302-8 lists 0.88 in. A -8 ORFS end is a different thread again, 13/16-16.

Why do two hoses with the same dash have different outside diameters?

The dash is the bore. The cover and the reinforcement change the outside. A -8 Parker 422 (SAE 100R1AT, one wire braid) lists OD 0.81 in. A -8 Parker 302 (SAE 100R2AT, two wire braids) lists 0.88 in. A -8 Gates EFG4K (marked as exceeding SAE 100R12, four spiral wires) lists 0.94 in. Use the OD from the catalog for the hose you are clamping or crimping. Do not read the dash off the outside.

Does SAE 100R5 use the same dash sizes?

No. Gates says the dash on SAE 100R5 hose (its C5 series), and on C14 and PolarSeal, is based on the tubing outside diameter, not on a true bore. Parker 201 is marked SAE J517 100R5. Parker lists dash -8 at 13/32 in inside, not 1/2 in. Parker says SAE spec certification for 201 hose is for -4 through -12 only.

Can I use the working pressure in the example table for any 100R2 hose?

No. Those pressures are the maximum working pressure Parker or Gates prints for the named hose. Parker 302-8 is 4000 psi. Gates EFG4K at -8 is also 4000 psi, and that hose is a spiral 100R12 type, not 100R2. Parker 722ST-20 is marked SAE 100R12 and is listed at 3000 psi, while Gates EFG4K at -20 is listed at 4000 psi. Read the layline and the catalog for the hose in your hand. Do not mix hose and fittings from different makers. Parker says to crimp only to the hose maker's specification.
