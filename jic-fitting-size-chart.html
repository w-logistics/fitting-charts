JIC Fitting Size Chart (SAE J514 / 37° Flare)

Standard dash sizes from -2 to -32 with tube OD, thread size, threads per inch, and nominal male-thread major diameter. Built for a quick lookup on a phone or tablet in the shop.

Quick rule: The dash size is the tube outside diameter in sixteenths of an inch. A -8 JIC is for 1/2″ tube and uses a 3/4-16 thread. That is not the thread on a -8 ORFS fitting, which is 13/16-16.

Thread callouts follow SAE J514 37° flare conventions. Sizes -2 through -10 use UNF (fine) threads. -12 and larger use 12-pitch UN threads. The male-thread diameters are the nominal major diameter of that thread size (9/16 in is 0.5625 in). A male thread measures slightly under that. Confirm with a thread gauge when you can.

How to Identify an Unknown JIC Fitting

  1. Look for the 37° flare — JIC uses a 37° seating surface on both male and female. This is the key visual difference from SAE 45° flare fittings, which look similar but seat at 45°.
  2. Measure the tube OD (or the nominal size the fitting is made for). That number in sixteenths is the dash size.
  3. Measure the male thread outside diameter with calipers and count threads per inch with a pitch gauge, or by counting threads in one inch.
  4. Match to the chart above. Thread size, TPI, and measured OD should line up with one row.
  5. Confirm it is not ORB or ORFS. ORB has an O-ring at the base of the male thread and no flare. ORFS has a flat face with an O-ring groove and no 37° cone. ORFS threads are a different series at the same dash size.

Common field notes

  • JIC is a metal-to-metal seal (flare to seat). It does not rely on an O-ring for the primary seal.
  • It is widely used on mobile equipment, agricultural machinery, and many industrial hydraulic lines in North America.
  • Do not mix JIC 37° with SAE 45°. The angles do not match and the joint will leak or damage the seats.
  • Do not mix JIC with ORFS. Some thread sizes appear in both families at different dash numbers (9/16-18 is -6 JIC and -4 ORFS), but the seats are not the same.
  • Pressure ratings drop as size increases. Check the manufacturer’s published working pressure for the specific fitting and material.

Quick Reference — Most Common Sizes

  • -4 → 1/4″ tube, 7/16-20
  • -6 → 3/8″ tube, 9/16-18
  • -8 → 1/2″ tube, 3/4-16 (very common)
  • -12 → 3/4″ tube, 1-1/16-12
  • -16 → 1″ tube, 1-5/16-12
Safety note: Wrong fitting type or size is a common cause of hydraulic leaks and failures. When in doubt, take measurements and compare to manufacturer data or a known good sample. This page is a reference aid, not a replacement for proper identification tools and specs.
