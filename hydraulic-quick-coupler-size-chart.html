Hydraulic Quick Coupler Size Chart: ISO 7241, ISO 16028, and ISO 5675

Body size, port thread, and how to tell the common hydraulic quick couplers apart: ISO 7241 Series A, ISO 7241 Series B, ISO 16028 flat face, and the ISO 5675 agricultural tip. Pressure and flow on this page belong to a named product series. They are not the rating of the ISO document.

Quick rule: A poppet nose is ISO 7241 Series A or Series B, or the agricultural tip. A flush face is ISO 16028. Series A and Series B are both poppet couplers, and they are not the same profile. The body size is not the pipe thread. A 1/2 in body is often sold with a 1/2 in port and with a 3/4 in port.

How to tell the types apart

ISO 7241:2023 specifies two series of hydraulic quick-action couplings. Series A is used predominantly in Europe and is preferred worldwide for agricultural and forestry machinery. The same document adds requirements for Series A when it is used in the agricultural applications of ISO 5675. Series B is used predominantly in North America and in the chemical industry. Parker Catalog 3800 still uses the older name ISO 7241-1 for those two series. Holmbury IA and IB sheets say they are made to ISO 7241:2014 Series A and Series B.

What separates the four profiles. Sleeve wording is the phrase Parker prints for that series.
Profile Valve Lock Sleeve Parker series
ISO 7241 Series A Poppet Ball Manual connect 6600 Series
ISO 7241 Series B Poppet Ball Manual sleeve 60 Series
ISO 16028 Flush face Ball Push to connect, Sleeve-Lok FEM Series
ISO 5675, 1/2 in Ball or poppet Ball 4000 Series: sleeve retracted to connect. 4200 Series: push to connect when the coupler is clamp mounted. 8010 nipple, 4000 and 4200 couplers

Series A and Series B both use a poppet and a ball lock. Pulling or pushing the sleeve does not name the series. Parker calls the 6600 sleeve Manual Connect and the 60 Series a manual sleeve. The faces that mate are different profiles. Use the male diameter in the Holmbury table below, or the series mark on the part.

A flat face is the one you can wipe clean across the nose. Parker calls the FEM valve flush and non-spill. ISO 16028:2023 is the flush-face standard. The abstract says couplings made to that document seal automatically on the upstream side and the downstream side when disconnected, and that they are not designed to connect or disconnect under pressure. Parker sells a separate FEC nipple that mates with FEM couplers and is built to connect with trapped pressure in the nipple. That nipple is not the basic ISO statement.

On the agricultural tip, ball and poppet are valve options in the same 8010 series. Parker lists 8010-4 as a ball valve and 8010-4P as a poppet valve, both 1/2 in body and both 1/2-14 NPTF. A ball valve in that series is not a flat-face coupler.

Which halves connect

  • Series A to Series A. Parker says the 6600 Series accepts ISO 7241-1 Series A nipples.
  • Series B to Series B. Parker says the 60 Series accepts ISO 7241-1 Series B nipples, and that the 60 Series complies with ISO 7241 Series B. The 60 Series is not listed as a mate for the 6600.
  • Flat face to flat face. Parker says FEM couplings have global interchangeability with other couplings that comply with ISO 16028. Do not mate a flush face to a poppet nose. They are different interfaces in the Parker catalog.
  • 1/2 in ISO 5675 tip to 1/2 in Series A. Parker says the 8010 nipple meets the dimensional requirements of ISO 5675 and will connect with all 1/2 in body size 4000, 4200, 5000, 6600, 8200, 8450, and 9200 couplers. The 6600 is the Series A coupler. The 60 Series is not on that list. Parker also says the 1/2 in 4000 coupler accepts ISO 5675 universal tips and accepts ISO 7241 nipples. Those two sentences are written for the 1/2 in size.
  • High-pressure flush face. Parker FH Series is a 3/8 in flush-face coupler with a red nipple body and a red sleeve. The catalog heading calls it HTMA high pressure. Rated pressure is 10,000 psi and rated flow is 6 gpm. Parker says it will not connect with lower pressure flush face couplings. It is not the FEM Series.

ISO 5675 agricultural tip, Parker 8010 and 4000

ISO 5675:2021 specifies the interface dimensions and the operating requirements for hydraulic couplers that transmit power from agricultural tractors to agricultural machinery. It applies to hydraulic lines other than braking circuits. Parker states the ISO 5675 dimensions on the 1/2 in 8010 nipple, and on the 1/2 in 4000 coupler. The 4000 Series also includes 1/4, 3/8, 3/4, and 1 in bodies. Parker does not call those other body sizes ISO 5675 in the feature list. The feature lines say the 1/2 in size accepts ISO 5675 universal tips and the 1/2 in size accepts ISO 7241 nipples.

The 8010 is a male tip. Catalog 3900 rates that 1/2 in body at 12 gpm and 3000 psi, nitrile, -40 F to +250 F. Parker says the nipples connect with the 1/2 in couplers listed in the interchange section above. Tips are ball, poppet, or a connect-under-pressure poppet. The connect-under-pressure parts are 8010-4P-DC (1/2-14 NPTF) and 8010-15P-DC (3/4-16 ORB). Parker says the DC version allows connection under system pressure on the coupler side and residual pressure up to 3000 psi on the nipple side, and that those DC nipples are not for use with 9200 Series couplers.

FAQ

Do ISO 7241 Series A and Series B couplers interchange?

No. ISO 7241:2023 describes two series. Parker Catalog 3800 puts them on different products. The 6600 Series accepts ISO 7241-1 Series A nipples. The 60 Series accepts ISO 7241-1 Series B nipples. Both are poppet valves with ball locking. Parker lists the 6600 sleeve as Manual Connect and the 60 Series as a manual sleeve, so the sleeve is not what splits them. Holmbury prints a different male diameter for IA (Series A) and IB (Series B) at the same nominal size. A 1/2 in IA male is 20.5 mm (0.81 in). A 1/2 in IB male is 23.5 mm (0.93 in). Match the series, then the body size.

Does a 1/2 in agricultural tip fit an ISO 7241 Series A coupler?

Parker says the 1/2 in tip does, and it does not say that for every body size. The 8010 nipple meets the dimensional requirements of ISO 5675. Parker says those nipples connect with all 1/2 in body size 4000, 4200, 5000, 6600, 8200, 8450, and 9200 series couplers. The 6600 Series is the Parker ISO 7241-1 Series A coupler. The 60 Series, which is Series B, is not in that list. The 4000 Series note that the coupler accepts ISO 5675 tips and ISO 7241 nipples is also written for the 1/2 in size.

How do I tell a flat-face coupler from a poppet coupler?

Look at the mating face. Parker 6600 and 60 Series couplers use a poppet valve. Parker FEM couplers use a flush face. Parker calls the FEM valve flush and non-spill, and the sleeve is push-to-connect with Sleeve-Lok. ISO 16028:2023 is the flush-face standard. It says those couplings seal on both sides when they are disconnected, and that they are not designed to connect or disconnect under pressure. A red 3/8 in Parker FH coupler is also flush face. Parker says the FH Series will not connect with lower pressure flush face couplings. A flat face and a red sleeve are not enough. Read the series.

Is the body size the same as the pipe thread?

No. Body size is the coupler series size. The port thread is separate, and one body size is sold with more than one thread. On the Parker 6600 Series, a 1/4 in body is listed with 1/8-27 NPTF or 1/4-18 NPTF. A 1/2 in body is listed with 1/2-14 NPTF or 3/4-14 NPTF, plus three ORB threads. On the Parker FEM Series, a 3/8 in body is listed with 3/8-18 NPTF and with 1/2-14 NPTF. Match the profile and the body size, then the port.

Can I use the pressure in these tables for any brand of the same ISO type?

No. The psi and gpm figures are the ratings Parker or Holmbury prints for the named series. Parker 6600 steel at 1/2 in is 4000 psi. Holmbury IA12, also a 1/2 in Series A coupler, is listed at 320 bar (4640 psi). Parker 60 Series steel at 1 in is 2000 psi on the dynamic rating, and the same table lists 3000 psi when the steel nipple is the heavy-duty version. ISO 16028:2023 describes flush-face couplings for use from 10 MPa (100 bar) to 31.5 MPa (315 bar), depending on size. That is the scope of the standard. Parker rates the FEM Series at 5000 psi and says that series meets or exceeds ISO 16028. Use the data sheet for the part you are installing.

Safety note: A coupler that threads into the hose and still will not latch is often the wrong series, not the wrong wrench size. Series A, Series B, flat face, and the agricultural tip do not all share one nose. Match the series and the body size, then the port thread, and use the pressure rating the manufacturer prints for that part, material, and seal.
